VPC and ECS at whiteboard depth

The conceptual model behind running containers on AWS, built for saying out loud rather than for clicking through. The network first: what a VPC is, why subnets exist, what actually makes a subnet public, the three ways out, and the two filters. Then the compute: what ECS is responsible for, who owns the machine underneath, and what happens to the network when a task starts. Closes on the reference architecture, walked hop by hop. Nine lessons, an interleaved quiz, a glossary, and a printable whiteboard sheet.

Arc 1 · The network

Addressing, placement, routing, exits, filtering. Five decisions that together decide what can reach what.

L1 What a VPC isA Region-scoped private network defined by an address range. Why the default VPC made all of this invisible, and what costs money. → L2 Subnets and Availability ZonesA subnet is an address range pinned to one zone. Why choosing a subnet is choosing a failure domain, and why diagrams show matched sets. → L3 Route tables and what public meansThere is no public flag. One route to an internet gateway is the whole difference, and the address is a separate decision. → L4 The three doors outInternet gateway, NAT gateway, VPC endpoint, told as one question: who is allowed to start the conversation. → L5 Security groups and network ACLsStateful allow-list on the resource against a stateless numbered list on the subnet. Plus the rule that names another group. →

Arc 2 · The compute

What ECS decides on your behalf, and how much of the machine you keep.

L6 What ECS is responsible forA control loop with four nouns: cluster, task definition, task, service. Desired count, and what the scheduler does about health. → L7 Who owns the boxFargate, EC2, Managed Instances, and ECS Anywhere as points on one axis. Launch types, capacity providers, and mixing them in one cluster. →

Arc 3 · Where they meet

The task joins the network, and the whole picture assembles.

L8 The task as a network citizenOne interface, one private IP, its own security groups. Why target groups use ip targets and how a task reaches the internet. → L9 The whiteboard walkthroughSynthesis. One request from client to database, every hop justified, plus the failure story to tell before being asked. →
